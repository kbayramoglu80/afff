Osteomyelitis (Bone Infection): Belirtiler, Tanı ve Tedavi

Written and medically reviewed by: Assoc. Prof. Dr. Mesut Karıksız, Orthopaedics and Traumatology Specialist · Last updated: November 15, 2026

Osteomyelitis (kemik enfeksiyonu) nedir? Osteomyelitis, kemiğin ve kemik iliğinin mikroplar (çoğunlukla bakteri) tarafından enfekte olmasıdır. Kana karışan mikroplarla (özellikle çocuklarda), açık kırık veya ameliyat sonrasında veya komşu dokudan yayılım ile gelişebilir. Belirtiler ateş, ağrı, şişlik, kızarıklık ve bazen akıntıdır. Tedavi genellikle uzun süreli antibiyotik ve gerektiğinde cerrahi temizleme (debridman) içerir; kronik olgularda kemik kaybı ve deformite için ileri rekonstrüksiyon gerekebilir.

How does it develop, and who is affected?

TypeDescription
Acute hematogenous (blood-borne)In children, often near the growth plates of long bones; germs reach the bloodstream from another infection
After trauma or surgeryAfter an open fracture, fracture surgery, or an implant; common in adults
ChronicPersistent bone infection due to untreated or inadequately treated infection; may involve dead bone (sequestrum), drainage (sinus tract), and intermittent flare-ups
Risk factorsDiabetes, immunosuppression, vascular disease, smoking, alcohol use, and bone implants

Symptoms

  • Fever, fatigue, and chills
  • Kemik üzerinde ağrı, hareket veya yük vermede artış; çocuklarda kolu ya da bacağı kullanmama, topallama
  • Swelling, redness, and warmth
  • Cilt altında irin veya akıntı (fistül); yara iyileşmemesi
  • Symptoms of chronic osteomyelitis may be mild or intermittent

A child with a fever who will not use a limb or has severe bone pain should be assessed without delay. Early treatment can help prevent lasting problems.

How is it diagnosed?

Muayene ve kan tetkikleri (CRP, sedimantasyon, beyaz küre) yapılır. Röntgen erken dönemde normal olabilir; MR erken tanıda en değerli yöntemlerdendir. Etkeni belirlemek için kan kültürü, doku veya sıvı örneği (biyopsi/aspirasyon) alınır. Antibiyotik başlamadan önce örnek alınması önemlidir. Enfeksiyon hastalıkları uzmanıyla birlikte değerlendirilir.

Treatment options

ConditionApproach
Acute osteomyelitisEarly, appropriate antibiotics for an adequate duration (initially through a vein); surgical drainage if the response is inadequate or an abscess is present
Chronic osteomyelitisSurgical removal of infected and dead bone and tissue (debridement), antibiotics, filling of the bone defect, and implant removal if needed
Bone loss and deformityBone transport (Ilizarov technique), bone grafting, or muscle and skin flaps in selected cases
Associated conditionsManagement of diabetes and vascular disease, smoking cessation, and nutritional support

Chronic osteomyelitis is difficult to treat and often requires more than one operation and prolonged antibiotic therapy. The goals are to control the infection and preserve limb function.

Treatment and follow-up

  • Antibiotic treatment may last weeks to months; its duration and route are determined with an infectious disease specialist
  • After surgery, close follow-up includes wound care, blood tests, and imaging
  • Reconstruction may take months in cases with bone loss
  • Long-term follow-up is recommended because recurrence may occur even years later

Possible risks and complications

  • Failure to fully control the infection or recurrence
  • Bone loss, fracture, non-union, and deformity
  • Growth plate damage and leg-length difference (in children)
  • Joint involvement (septic arthritis) and joint stiffness
  • Antibiotic side effects (kidney or liver effects, diarrhoea, etc.)
  • Rarely, widespread infection (sepsis) or loss of the limb

Bu riskler uygun hasta seçimi, dikkatli teknik ve düzenli kontrollerle azaltılmaya çalışılır. Sizin için geçerli olanlar muayenede ayrıntılı konuşulur.

When should you see an orthopaedic specialist?

  • Bone or joint pain, swelling, or redness with fever
  • A child avoiding use of an arm or leg, or limping
  • Drainage, redness, and pain at a surgical wound or fracture site
  • A non-healing wound or sinus tract with intermittent drainage

Frequently asked questions

What is osteomyelitis, and is it contagious?

Osteomyelitis is a bone infection. It is not usually spread from person to person; germs reach the bone from another source in the body, a wound, or a surgical site.

What are the signs of a bone infection in children?

Signs may include fever, severe bone pain, avoiding use of an arm or leg, limping, swelling, and redness. Prompt assessment is needed if these occur.

Can osteomyelitis be treated with antibiotics alone?

Antibiotics alone may be sufficient for acute cases diagnosed early. Surgical debridement is also needed if there is an abscess or dead bone, and for chronic cases.

Can chronic osteomyelitis be cured completely?

The infection can be controlled in many patients with appropriate surgical debridement and antibiotics. However, long-term follow-up is needed because recurrence is possible; outcomes vary by patient and coexisting conditions.

Is an infection around an implant after surgery osteomyelitis?

An infection around an implant (such as a screw or plate) is considered osteomyelitis if it spreads to the bone. Treatment often involves removing or replacing the implant, surgical debridement, and antibiotics.

What is done if a bone infection causes bone loss?

After the infection is cleared, reconstructive approaches such as bone transport (Ilizarov), bone grafting, or a flap may be considered for bone loss. The choice depends on the extent of bone loss and the patient.

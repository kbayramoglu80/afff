Meniscal Tear: Symptoms, Diagnosis, and Treatment

Written and medically reviewed by: Assoc. Prof. Dr. Mesut Karıksız, Orthopaedics and Traumatology Specialist · Last updated: November 15, 2026

What is a meniscal tear? The menisci are C-shaped pieces of cartilage between the thighbone and shinbone that distribute load and absorb shock. They can tear during twisting movements in sports or due to age-related wear. Symptoms include knee pain, swelling, and catching or locking. Treatment may be non-surgical or arthroscopic, depending on the tear location and type and the person age.

What causes it?

  • Sudden twisting with the foot planted or deep squatting during sports
  • Age-related wear (degenerative tears), which may occur without a specific injury
  • May occur together with other knee injuries, such as an ACL injury

Symptoms

  • Pain along the inner or outer joint line
  • Swelling
  • A sensation of catching, locking, or popping in the knee
  • Pain when squatting, using stairs, or twisting
  • Inability to fully straighten or bend the knee

How is it diagnosed?

Diagnosis begins with the history and specific examination tests. MRI helps locate and characterize the tear and assess associated ligament or cartilage damage. X-rays help evaluate the bones and signs of osteoarthritis.

Treatment options

OptionWhen is it considered?How is it performed?
Non-surgical treatmentSmall tears, mild symptoms, no locking, and especially tears associated with age-related wearPhysical therapy, muscle strengthening, activity modification, medication, and injections for some patients at the doctor discretion
Arthroscopic meniscal repairTear types suitable for repair, particularly in young and active patientsThe tear is sutured using a minimally invasive approach to preserve meniscal tissue
Arthroscopic partial meniscectomyTears that cannot be repaired or cause lockingOnly the damaged portion is removed, preserving healthy tissue

The aim is to preserve as much meniscal tissue as possible because it helps protect the joint cartilage. Decisions consider the tear type and location, age, activity level, and associated injuries.

Recovery after surgery

After meniscal repair, weight-bearing and knee bending may be restricted for a time, and rehabilitation proceeds more cautiously. Recovery is usually faster after partial meniscectomy. The timeline varies with the procedure and individual.

Frequently asked questions

Can a meniscal tear heal on its own?

Blood supply varies across the meniscus. Some tears in the better-supplied outer area may heal, while tears in the inner area are less likely to heal spontaneously. Small, symptom-free tears can be monitored without surgery. Decisions are based on examination and MRI findings.

For a meniscal tear ameliyat şart mı?

No. Tears with mild symptoms, no locking, and especially those related to age-related wear may be managed with physical therapy. Surgery may be considered for knee locking, recurrent catching, or persistent pain despite non-surgical treatment.

What is the difference between meniscal repair and removal?

Meniscal repair stitches the tear to preserve tissue and usually involves a more cautious recovery. Partial meniscectomy removes the damaged portion and often allows faster mobilization, but some tissue is lost. The appropriate option depends on the tear’s type and location and the patient.

When can I walk after meniscus surgery?

The timeline depends on the procedure. Most patients can walk soon after partial meniscectomy, while weight-bearing may be restricted for a time after repair. Your doctor will provide a specific plan after surgery.

What happens if a meniscal tear is untreated?

Small tears without symptoms may not cause problems. However, tears that cause locking or enlarge can lead to persistent pain and may accelerate joint cartilage wear. An assessment is recommended if symptoms continue.

With a meniscal tear ön çapraz bağ yaralanması birlikte olur mu?

Yes, the two often occur together in sports injuries. MRI is used to assess both structures and guide treatment.

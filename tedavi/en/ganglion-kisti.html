Ganglion Cyst and Surgical Treatment

Written and medically reviewed by: Doç. Dr. Mesut Karıksız, Orthopedics and Traumatology Specialist · Last updated: November 15, 2026

What is a ganglion cyst? A ganglion cyst is a benign, jelly-like fluid-filled lump connected to a joint or tendon sheath. It most often develops around the wrist and sometimes on the fingers or foot. It may affect appearance and sometimes cause pain or pressure. Cysts without symptoms can be monitored; aspiration or surgical removal may be considered if they are painful or troublesome.

Where does it occur?

LocationFeatures
Back of the wristThe most common location; a round lump on the back of the wrist
Palm side of the wristMay be close to the radial artery and requires care during surgery
Base of the finger (palm side)A small, firm, painful cyst arising from the tendon sheath
End joint of the finger (mucous cyst)Usually associated with arthritis; it may cause a groove or deformity around the nail

Symptoms

  • A usually painless lump on the hand or wrist lump
  • Its size may change; it may sometimes shrink or disappear
  • Pain or weakness when the wrist is strained
  • Numbness and tingling if it presses on nerves
  • Tenderness while gripping with cysts at the base of a finger

How is it diagnosed?

Diagnosis is usually made by examination; the cyst is typically soft, mobile, and transilluminates when light is shone through it. Ultrasound Ultrasound shows the cyst contents and its relationship to nearby vessels and nerves. MRI may be used for occult ganglia or when the diagnosis is uncertain. MRI MRI may be used. Tissue removed in uncertain cases may be examined.

Treatment options

OptionWhen is it considered?Notes
ObservationCysts that do not cause pain or functional impairmentSome shrink or disappear on their own
Needle aspirationFor painful or bothersome cysts, or for patients who prefer not to have surgeryStraightforward, but the cyst may refill (recurrence is common)
Surgical removalPain, nerve compression, functional impairment, or recurring cystsThe cyst and its base are removed together; this can be done with open or arthroscopic techniques

Crushing the cyst with a hard object is not recommended; it may cause injury and does not provide a lasting solution.

After surgery

Surgery is usually performed under local or regional anesthesia, and most patients go home the same day. Wrist movement may be limited for a time, followed by exercises to resume daily activities. Return to strenuous work is determined individually.

Potential risks

  • The cyst may recur, even after surgery
  • Tenderness at the surgical site, scarring, and wrist stiffness
  • General surgical risks such as infection and bleeding
  • Nerve or blood-vessel injury (particularly with cysts on the palm side)

When should you see an orthopedic specialist?

  • A growing lump on the wrist or finger
  • Pain, weakness, or limited movement
  • Numbness and tingling
  • Firm, rapidly growing, or fixed masses

Frequently asked questions

Can a ganglion cyst go away on its own?

Some ganglion cysts shrink or disappear over time. Cysts that cause no pain or functional problems can be monitored. An examination is recommended for cysts that grow or cause symptoms.

Is a ganglion cyst dangerous?

Ganglion cysts are benign and are not cancer. However, not every wrist lump is a ganglion, so examination and imaging if needed are required for a definitive diagnosis.

Will needle aspiration cure a ganglion cyst?

Aspiration is straightforward, but the cyst often refills. Surgery may therefore be considered for a more lasting solution.

When is surgery for a ganglion cyst needed?

It may be considered for pain, nerve compression, functional impairment, frequent recurrence, or concern about appearance. The decision is based on examination findings.

Can a ganglion return after surgery?

Yes, recurrence can occur after surgery. The rate varies with the cyst location and surgical technique.

When can I use my hand after ganglion surgery?

Light daily use is usually allowed early. The clinician determines when to resume heavy lifting and activities that strain the wrist, based on the procedure performed.

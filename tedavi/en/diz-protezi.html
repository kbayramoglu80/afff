Knee Replacement (Total Knee Arthroplasty): Candidates, Procedure, and Recovery

Written and medically reviewed by: Assoc. Prof. Dr. Mesut Karıksız, Orthopaedics and Traumatology Specialist · Last updated: November 15, 2026

What is knee replacement surgery? Total knee arthroplasty replaces worn joint surfaces with metal and specialised plastic implants. It aims to reduce pain and make daily movement easier and may be considered for advanced osteoarthritis when non-surgical treatments have not provided enough relief.

When is it performed?

  • Advanced knee osteoarthritis with significant narrowing of the joint space
  • Knee pain that limits daily activities, walking, or sleep
  • Persistent symptoms despite non-surgical treatment such as medication, weight management, physiotherapy, or injections
  • Inflammatory joint diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis, or joint damage after a fracture
  • Significant knee deformity, such as bow-legged alignment

Knee replacement is considered alongside a person's symptoms, limitations in daily life, and expectations—not on the basis of X-ray findings alone.

Preoperative assessment

  • Examination and standing knee X-rays; MRI or other imaging if needed
  • Blood tests and cardiopulmonary and anaesthesia assessments
  • Control of diabetes, high blood pressure, and other conditions, and review of current medications
  • Smoking cessation, weight management where appropriate, and treatment of dental or urinary infections
  • Preoperative exercises and a plan for returning home

  2. Preparation: Damaged cartilage and a thin layer of bone are removed to shape the ends of the femur and tibia.
  3. Implant placement: Metal components are fixed to the bone, with durable polyethylene between them. The back of the kneecap may also be resurfaced if needed.
  4. Checks and closure: Knee movement and balance are checked, then the wound is closed.

Recovery

  • Most patients start standing on the day of surgery or within the first few days; early movement helps reduce clot risk and supports recovery
  • Physiotherapy and exercise usually begin soon after surgery and continue for several weeks
  • A walker or crutches may be used at first; pain and swelling are managed
  • Returning to daily activities usually takes weeks, while full recovery may take months

How long does a knee replacement last?

Longevity varies with the implant, body weight, activity level, and surgical technique. Many knee replacements function for years. Revision surgery may be needed if the implant wears or loosens over time.

Potential risks

  • Infection, including serious infection around the implant
  • Blood clots in the leg or lung
  • Knee stiffness or limited movement
  • Persistent pain or pain greater than expected
  • Implant loosening, wear, or breakage over time
  • Rare nerve or blood-vessel injury, or differences in leg length or alignment

These risks are addressed through appropriate patient selection, preparation, careful technique, and follow-up. Your individual risks can be discussed during an assessment.

When should I see an orthopaedic specialist?

  • Knee pain that interferes with walking, using stairs, or sleeping
  • Insufficient relief from non-surgical treatment
  • Rapidly worsening deformity or persistent swelling
  • After a previous replacement: pain, swelling, fever, or redness

Frequently asked questions

Who may need a knee replacement?

It may be considered for people with advanced knee osteoarthritis, pain that significantly limits daily activities, and insufficient relief from non-surgical treatment. The decision is based on symptoms, examination, and X-ray findings.

Is there an age limit for knee replacement?

There is no single age limit. The decision depends on symptom severity, general health, and expectations rather than age alone. Alternatives and implant longevity are also considered for younger patients.

When can I walk after knee replacement?

Most patients begin standing and walking with a walker or crutches within the first few days after surgery. Early movement is important, and progress is gradual with physiotherapy.

How long does knee replacement surgery take?

The duration varies by patient and procedure. Your care team can discuss the expected timing before surgery.

How long does a knee replacement last?

Longevity varies with the implant, body weight, activity level, and surgical technique. Many knee replacements function for years; revision surgery may be needed if the implant loosens or wears over time.

Which activities can I do after knee replacement?

Many people can return to low-impact activities such as walking, cycling, and swimming. A clinician should assess whether running, jumping, or other high-impact sports are appropriate.

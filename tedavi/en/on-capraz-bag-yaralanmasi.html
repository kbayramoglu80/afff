Anterior Cruciate Ligament Injury: Belirtiler, Tanı ve Tedavi

Written and medically reviewed by: Assoc. Prof. Dr. Mesut Karıksız, Orthopaedics and Traumatology Specialist · Last updated: November 15, 2026

Anterior cruciate ligament injury nedir? The anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) is inside the knee joint and helps stabilize the knee, particularly during twisting movements. It is often torn during sports involving sudden turns, stops, or landing from a jump. Symptoms include sudden pain, swelling, and a feeling that the knee is giving way. Treatment may be surgical or non-surgical, depending on the tear and activity level.

What causes it?

  • Sudden direction changes, pivoting, or stopping in sports such as football, basketball, volleyball, or skiing
  • The knee collapsing inward when landing from a jump
  • High-energy trauma, such as a direct blow or road traffic collision

Symptoms

  • Sometimes hearing a pop at the time of injury
  • Rapid onset of knee swelling and pain
  • A feeling that the knee is giving way, slipping, or unstable
  • Dizi tam bükememe veya tam açamama
  • Yürürken veya merdivende dizin tutmaması

How is it diagnosed?

Diagnosis begins with the history and physical examination; specific tests assess ligament laxity. MRI shows the ligament tear and possible meniscal or cartilage damage. X-rays help rule out associated fractures.

Possible risks

  • Enfeksiyon, kanama ve pıhtı oluşumu gibi genel ameliyat riskleri
  • Diz hareketlerinde sertlik
  • Greft alınan bölgede ağrı veya his değişikliği
  • Greftin yeniden yırtılması veya gevşemesi

Bu riskler uygun teknik, aşamalı rehabilitasyon ve düzenli kontrollerle azaltılmaya çalışılır. Sizin için geçerli olan riskler muayenede ayrıntılı konuşulur.

When should you see an orthopaedic specialist?

  • Spor veya düşme sonrası dizde hızlı şişlik ve ağrı
  • Dizin boşalması veya tutmaması hissi
  • Dizin tam açılmaması veya kilitlenmesi
  • Geçmeyen veya tekrarlayan diz burkulmaları

Frequently asked questions

Can an anterior cruciate ligament tear heal on its own?

A completely torn ACL usually does not heal on its own. However, not every tear requires surgery; partial tears or people with lower activity levels may be managed with physical therapy. Decisions are based on examination and MRI findings.

Is surgery always needed for an ACL tear?

No. Surgery is more often recommended if the knee feels unstable, work or sports require turning and pivoting, or other structures are also injured. Non-surgical treatment may be an option for people with lower activity levels.

How is ACL surgery performed?

Surgery is usually performed arthroscopically through small incisions. A tendon graft, often taken from the patient’s own tissue, replaces the damaged ligament. Graft selection varies by patient and surgeon.

When can I return to sports after surgery?

Timing varies by patient and sport. Rehabilitation progresses in stages and usually takes months; returning to sports that involve turning and pivoting takes longer. The specific timeline is determined through follow-up and strength testing.

Can a meniscal tear also occur with an ACL tear?

Yes. Meniscal and cartilage injuries can occur with ACL injuries. Other structures are therefore assessed by examination and MRI, and the treatment plan is tailored accordingly.

Can an untreated ACL tear cause problems?

If the knee remains unstable, recurrent giving-way episodes, meniscal injury, and gradual wear of the joint cartilage may occur. Risk varies from person to person and should be assessed clinically.

Microsurgical Nerve and Blood Vessel Repair

Written and medically reviewed by: Assoc. Prof. Mesut Kariksiz, Orthopaedic Surgeon · Last updated: November 15, 2026

What is microsurgical nerve and blood vessel repair? Microsurgery uses an operating microscope and very fine sutures to repair structures only millimeters in size. It is used to repair severed or crushed nerves and blood vessels. Nerve ends may be sutured directly; if there is a gap, a nerve graft or transfer may be needed. Nerves heal very slowly, so the outcome may take months or years to become clear. Early diagnosis and timely repair affect the result.

When is it performed?

  • Severed nerves caused by knives, glass, or machinery
  • Nerve injuries associated with crushing, stretching, fractures, or dislocations
  • Arterial and venous injuries; amputation and replantation (reattachment of a part)
  • Cases requiring removal and repair because of a mass or scar on a nerve
  • Nerve grafts and transfers in selected patients

Symptoms

  • Loss of sensation or numbness in the hand, fingers, or arm after an injury
  • Weakness when lifting or moving a finger, wrist, or foot (muscle paralysis)
  • Muscle wasting and pain after nerve injury
  • Bleeding, coldness, color change, and loss of pulse after a blood vessel injury (emergency)

How is it diagnosed?

Diagnosis is based on the history and examination, including tests of sensation, muscle strength, and circulation in the fingers. In open injuries, nerve and vessel status can often be seen directly during surgery. For closed injuries, EMG (nerve conduction testing) and ultrasound or MRI can assess the nerve; EMG findings become more reliable some time after injury. Doppler ultrasound or angiography may be needed for blood vessel injuries.

Recovery

  • Nerves heal very slowly (roughly 1 mm per day); it takes a long time for regrowth to reach the muscle or skin.
  • The area is protected with a splint for a period after repair; hand therapy and rehabilitation then begin.
  • Sensory re-education is used to support return of sensation, and an exercise program to support muscle function.
  • After blood vessel repair, circulation is monitored closely; smoking should be stopped.

Possible risks and expectations

  • Function may not return completely; outcomes vary with age, injury type and level, and timing of repair.
  • Pain, sensitivity to cold, or a small lump at the nerve end (neuroma)
  • Blockage (thrombosis) after blood vessel repair and the need for further surgery
  • Infection, bleeding, or wound-healing problems
  • Stiffness in muscles and joints that have not been used for a long time

When should you seek medical care?

  • Loss of sensation, numbness, or movement weakness after an injury: seek medical attention promptly
  • Color change, coolness, or bleeding in a finger or hand after a cut: emergency care
  • Nerve symptoms following a fracture or dislocation
  • Sensation or movement that has not improved after previous repair

Frequently asked questions

Can a severed nerve heal on its own?

A completely severed nerve usually does not reconnect properly on its own and requires surgical repair. In crush or stretch injuries, recovery may occur spontaneously if the nerve remains intact; follow-up and examination are therefore important.

When should nerve repair be performed?

Repair is usually performed early for open, clean cuts. For closed injuries, a period of observation and EMG assessment may come before a decision. Timing depends on the type of injury.

Sinir ne kadar sürede iyileşir?

Sinirler çok yavaş iyileşir; kabaca günde yaklaşık 1 mm ilerler. Hasar seviyesi hedef kasa veya deriden uzaksa iyileşme aylar, hatta yıllar sürebilir. Sonuç hastadan hastaya değişir.

Sinir onarımından sonra his tamamen geri döner mi?

Tam his ve güç dönüşü her hastada olmayabilir. Genç yaşta, temiz kesilerde, zamanında yapılan onarımlarda sonuçlar daha iyi olma eğilimindedir; ancak garanti verilemez.

Blood vessel repair neden bu kadar önemlidir?

When an artery is cut, blood supply to the tissues is disrupted; without repair, the limb may lose viability. Blood vessel injuries are therefore emergencies and require prompt medical attention.

Is rehabilitation needed after microsurgery?

Yes. Hand therapy, splint use, range-of-motion and strengthening exercises, and sensory re-education are important parts of recovery. The program is tailored to the repair performed.

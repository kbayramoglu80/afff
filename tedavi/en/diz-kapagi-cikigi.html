Patellar Dislocation: Symptoms, Diagnosis, and Treatment

Written and medically reviewed by: Assoc. Prof. Dr. Mesut Karıksız, Orthopaedics and Traumatology Specialist · Last updated: November 15, 2026

What is a patellar dislocation? The kneecap normally glides in a groove at the end of the thighbone. A dislocation occurs when it moves out of the groove, usually outward, causing sudden pain and swelling. First-time dislocations are often treated without surgery; surgery may be considered for recurrent dislocations or associated injuries.

What causes it?

  • Twisting or changing direction during sport, or a direct blow to the kneecap
  • Predisposing anatomy, such as a shallow groove, ligament laxity, or leg-alignment differences
  • A previous dislocation

Symptoms

  • Sudden, severe knee pain
  • The kneecap can be seen or felt moving out of place, usually outward
  • Rapid swelling
  • Inability to bend the knee or bear weight
  • In recurrent cases, a sensation that the kneecap is “slipping” or may dislocate

How is it diagnosed?

Assessment begins with an examination and X-rays, which show whether there is a fracture and the position of the kneecap. MRI is used to assess cartilage that may have been injured during the dislocation and the medial patellofemoral ligament (MPFL), which helps keep the kneecap in place.

Treatment options

ConditionManagement
First dislocation without fracture or a loose cartilage fragmentUsually non-surgical: reduction, short-term protection, physiotherapy, and muscle strengthening
Recurrent dislocation or persistent instabilitySurgery may be considered, usually MPFL reconstruction; additional procedures may be needed for predisposing anatomy
Associated fracture or loose cartilage fragmentSurgery may be needed to repair or remove the fragment

The MPFL helps keep the kneecap on the inner side and is often torn during dislocation. Treatment depends on age, number of dislocations, predisposing anatomy, and associated injuries.

Recovery

Non-surgical treatment protects the knee for a short period, followed by progressive physiotherapy to restore muscle strength and knee control. Rehabilitation after surgery is tailored to the procedure and usually takes months.

Potential risks

  • Recurrent dislocation
  • Front-of-knee pain and cartilage wear around the kneecap
  • Knee stiffness
  • General surgical risks such as infection, bleeding, and blood clots

When should I see an orthopaedic specialist?

  • If the kneecap has dislocated or you are unsure whether it is in place, seek medical care as soon as possible
  • An examination is needed even if the kneecap has returned to place on its own
  • Repeated slipping or a feeling that the kneecap may dislocate again
  • Persistent swelling or pain after a dislocation

Frequently asked questions

Does a patellar dislocation require surgery?

First-time dislocations are usually treated without surgery when there is no fracture or loose cartilage fragment. Surgery may be considered for recurrent dislocations, persistent instability, or associated injury. The decision is based on examination and MRI findings.

Can the kneecap dislocate again?

The risk of recurrence varies. It is higher in people who have their first dislocation at a young age or have predisposing anatomy. Strengthening and, when needed, surgery aim to reduce this risk.

What is the MPFL?

The MPFL helps hold the kneecap on the inner side and is often torn during dislocation. Reconstructing this ligament is a common surgical option for recurrent dislocations.

When can I return to sport after a dislocation?

Timing varies by treatment and individual. A gradual return is considered once pain and swelling have settled and knee movement and muscle strength are adequate. The schedule is determined during follow-up.

Is patellar dislocation different in children and adolescents?

Recurrence may be more likely in younger patients, and treatment requires special consideration because of the growth plates. Assessment by an orthopaedic specialist is recommended for children and adolescents.

What should I do if my kneecap dislocates?

Support the knee without moving it, apply ice, and seek medical care as soon as possible. Do not try to put the kneecap back in place yourself.

Sanakin Therapy: What Is It and Who May Be a Candidate?

Written and medically reviewed by: Assoc. Prof. Mesut Kariksiz, Orthopaedic Surgeon · Last updated: November 15, 2026

What is Sanakin therapy? Sanakin is a serum-based autologous biologic injection prepared from a patient’s own blood and is a trade name. It is used as an adjunctive treatment for joint pain, such as knee osteoarthritis. Evidence specific to this treatment may be limited compared with more extensively studied options such as PRP. It does not replace standard treatment; expectations should be realistic, and decisions should follow a medical assessment.

Önemli bilgilendirme

Sanakin is a biologic treatment prepared from the patient’s own blood; it is not a standard orthopaedic treatment. Its effects vary by condition and individual. This page is for general information; claims that it is a “definitive cure” or “regenerates cartilage” have not been scientifically proven.

How is Sanakin prepared?

A small amount of blood is collected and processed in a laboratory to obtain serum. The product is then injected into the same patient. Because it is prepared from the patient’s own blood, the risk of transmitting infection or causing an allergic reaction is low. Preparation details may vary by system and protocol.

When is it used?

  • Mild to moderate knee osteoarthritis and joint pain
  • As an adjunctive treatment for selected joint problems, such as in the shoulder or hip
  • Joint pain after sports injuries and some soft-tissue problems
  • May be considered as an option when non-surgical treatments have not provided enough benefit

Although orthobiologic treatments are often presented as non-surgical approaches using a patient’s own tissues, they do not have the same effect for every condition or patient. Expectations should be realistic, and treatment decisions should be based on examination and imaging findings.

How does it differ from PRP?

PRP is a more extensively studied orthopaedic method that injects the platelet-rich portion of blood. Sanakin is a serum-based product with a different preparation method and composition. Both are derived from the patient’s own blood. The appropriate option depends on the condition, the patient, and available evidence.

Who may not be a candidate?

  • People with an active infection or a skin infection at the treatment site
  • People with bleeding or clotting disorders or taking blood thinners (medical assessment is needed)
  • People with a febrile illness, severe anemia, or active cancer (medical assessment is needed)
  • Benefit may be limited in joints with advanced osteoarthritis

How is it administered?

  1. Blood draw and preparation: Blood is collected and serum is prepared.
  2. Preparation: The treatment area is cleaned; local anesthesia may be used if needed.
  3. Injection: The product is injected into the joint or relevant area, often with ultrasound guidance to help target the intended site.
  4. Aftercare: Brief observation; most patients go home the same day. The session plan is individualized.

Evidence

The effects of biologic products prepared from a patient’s own blood vary with the preparation method, condition, and individual. Research specific to Sanakin may be limited compared with more extensively studied options such as PRP, and high-quality long-term comparative data may not always be available. It should therefore be considered an additional option for selected patients, not a replacement for standard treatment.

Possible side effects and risks

  • Temporary pain, swelling, and tenderness at the injection site
  • Enfeksiyon (nadir)
  • Lack of expected benefit or short duration of effect
  • Kanama ve morarma

Since the product is prepared from the patient’s own blood, allergic reactions are uncommon; infection remains a risk with any injection.

When should you see an orthopaedic specialist?

  • Knee or joint pain that persists despite non-surgical treatments
  • If you would like to learn which orthobiologic treatment may be suitable for you
  • Increasing swelling, redness, warmth, or fever after treatment

Frequently asked questions

What is Sanakin therapy?

Sanakin is an autologous, serum-based biologic injection prepared from the patient’s own blood. It may be used as an adjunct for joint pain, but it is not a standard orthopaedic treatment.

Can Sanakin cure knee osteoarthritis?

It has not been proven to eliminate osteoarthritis or regenerate cartilage. Some patients may experience pain relief, but responses vary.

How does Sanakin differ from PRP?

Both are prepared from the patient’s own blood. PRP injects the platelet-rich portion and has been studied more extensively; Sanakin is serum-based. The appropriate choice depends on the condition and patient.

How many Sanakin sessions are given?

The number and spacing of sessions depend on the condition and protocol. Your physician will determine the plan after an assessment.

Does Sanakin injection have side effects?

Temporary pain, swelling, and tenderness may occur after injection. Since it is prepared from the patient’s own blood, allergic reactions are uncommon; infection is rare.

What should I consider when deciding about Sanakin?

Ask your physician whether it is appropriate for your condition, as well as about expected benefits, limitations, risks, alternatives, and cost. If you do not receive clear answers, consider a second opinion.

Pes Planus (Flatfoot): Belirtiler, Tanı ve Tedavi

Written and medically reviewed by: Assoc. Prof. Dr. Mesut Karıksız, Orthopaedics and Traumatology Specialist · Last updated: November 15, 2026

What is pes planus (flatfoot)? Pes planus, or flatfoot, is a low arch along the inner edge of the foot that may disappear when standing or walking. It is flexible and painless in many children; the arch develops during childhood, and treatment is often unnecessary. Assessment is appropriate for pain, fatigue, ankle symptoms, or adult-onset flatfoot. Treatment may include insoles, exercises, and surgery when needed.

Types of flatfoot

TypeFeatures
Flexible flatfootThe arch flattens when standing and reappears when rising onto the toes or sitting. This is the most common type in children
Rigid flatfootThe arch does not form in any position, and foot movement is restricted. Causes such as a bony connection (tarsal coalition) may be present and should be assessed
Adult-acquired flatfootOften associated with dysfunction of the tibialis posterior tendon on the inner side of the foot; it may cause pain and progressive deformity

What causes it?

  • Structural and familial characteristics, joint laxity
  • An arch that has not yet developed in children
  • Tibialis posterior tendon dysfunction (in adults)
  • Excess weight, trauma, and inflammatory joint disease
  • Structural causes such as congenital bony fusion (tarsal coalition)

Symptoms

  • A low or absent arch along the inner edge of the foot
  • The heel turning outward when standing
  • Foot and ankle pain and fatigue after prolonged walking or standing
  • Swelling and tenderness on the inner side of the foot, especially in adults
  • Wear along the inner edge of shoes
  • In children, occasional leg or knee pain or frequent falls

How is it diagnosed?

The foot is examined while standing and walking; an arch that reappears when rising onto the toes usually indicates flexible flatfoot. Weight-bearing X-rays show foot bone alignment. CT or MRI may provide further information if rigid flatfoot or a tendon problem is suspected.

Treatment options

ConditionApproach
Painless flexible flatfoot (children and adults)Treatment is usually unnecessary; observation is sufficient. Insoles are not routinely recommended to correct the arch when there is no pain
Painful flatfootInsoles or orthoses, supportive footwear, calf (Achilles) stretching and foot-strengthening exercises, weight management, and medication if needed
Progressive deformity or lack of response to non-surgical treatmentSurgery may include an implant to limit joint motion in selected children, or tendon transfer, heel bone (calcaneal) osteotomy, or joint fusion in adults

Insoles do not permanently correct flatfoot; they aim to reduce symptoms and balance pressure. Treatment is individualized according to age, symptoms, and deformity type.

Recovery

With non-surgical treatment, symptoms often improve over several weeks; insoles and exercises are continued together. After surgery, the foot is protected in a cast or boot for a period, and weight-bearing depends on the procedure. Full recovery may take months.

Possible risks

  • General surgical risks such as infection, bleeding, and blood clots
  • Foot stiffness or pain that does not fully resolve
  • Partial recurrence of the deformity or inadequate/overcorrection
  • Pain related to an implant placed in children (with some techniques)
  • Delayed bone healing

When should you see an orthopaedic specialist?

  • Foot or ankle pain, or easy fatigue
  • Swelling along the inner side of the foot, arch collapse, or a change in foot posture in adults
  • Pain that limits standing or walking, one-sided flatfoot, or a rigid foot in a child
  • Excessive shoe wear and frequent sprains

Frequently asked questions

Is flatfoot normal in children?

The arch has not yet developed in many young children and becomes more noticeable as they grow. Painless, flexible flatfoot usually does not cause problems. Assessment is recommended for pain, a rigid foot, one-sided symptoms, or difficulty walking.

Can insoles correct flatfoot?

Insoles do not permanently create an arch, but may reduce pain and fatigue by redistributing pressure. Insoles are generally not needed for painless, flexible flatfoot. Use is decided based on an assessment.

Is surgery needed for flatfoot?

Most patients do not need surgery. It may be considered for selected patients with persistent pain despite non-surgical treatment, progressive deformity, or tendon dysfunction.

What causes adult-acquired flatfoot?

The most common cause is dysfunction of the tibialis posterior tendon, which supports the arch on the inner side of the foot. The arch may gradually collapse and become painful. Early assessment may provide more treatment options.

Does flatfoot prevent sports?

Many people with painless flatfoot can play sports. If pain or fatigue occurs, assessment is recommended, with insoles or exercises if needed.

How is flatfoot assessed for military service?

Assessment for military service depends on current health regulations and a medical board examination. For individual guidance, consult a doctor and the official regulations.

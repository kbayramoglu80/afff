Pes Cavus (High-Arched Foot): Belirtiler, Tanı ve Tedavi

Written and medically reviewed by: Assoc. Prof. Dr. Mesut Karıksız, Orthopaedics and Traumatology Specialist · Last updated: November 15, 2026

What is pes cavus (high-arched foot)? Pes cavus is a foot deformity in which the arch along the inner edge of the foot is higher than usual, placing much of the load on the heel and forefoot when walking. It may cause pain along the outer edge, recurrent ankle sprains, and calluses. Examination is important because some cases may be associated with an underlying neurological condition. Treatment ranges from insoles and orthoses to surgery.

What causes it?

  • Nörolojik nedenler: Neuromuscular conditions such as Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease, and spinal cord or nerve disorders
  • Travma sonrası (ayak ve bacak yaralanmalarının ardından kas ve kemik dengesinin bozulması)
  • Deformities such as congenital clubfoot and residual changes after treatment
  • No clear cause is found in some patients (idiopathic)

High-arched foot, özellikle ilerleyen, tek taraflı olduğunda veya ayak bileğinde güçsüzlük (düşük ayak) eşlik ettiğinde altta yatan nörolojik bir hastalık açısından araştırılmalıdır. Her çukur ayağın nörolojik nedeni olmaz.

Symptoms

  • A visibly high arch
  • Pain along the outer edge of the foot, heel, and forefoot; calluses on the sole
  • Frequent ankle sprains and ankle instability
  • Hammering or clawing of the toes
  • Difficulty wearing shoes
  • Weakness when lifting the foot (foot drop)

How is it diagnosed?

Examination assesses foot posture, hindfoot alignment, and ankle stability. The Coleman block test helps determine whether the hindfoot deformity is flexible and guides treatment. Weight-bearing X-rays assess bone alignment. If a neurological cause is suspected, further evaluation may include a neurological examination, EMG, neurology consultation, or MRI.

After surgery

After surgery, the foot is protected in a cast or boot for a period, and weight-bearing depends on the procedure. Physical therapy then focuses on movement, balance, and strength. Full recovery may take months.

Possible risks

  • Recurrence of the deformity or inadequate correction
  • Foot stiffness or pain that does not fully resolve
  • Delayed bone healing
  • General surgical risks such as infection, bleeding, and blood clots
  • Nerve injury or altered skin sensation

When should you see an orthopaedic specialist?

  • Persistent pain along the outer edge of the foot or heel
  • Recurrent ankle sprains
  • Foot weakness, inability to lift the foot, or catching the toes while walking
  • A deformity that worsens over time, especially if one-sided or in a child

Frequently asked questions

What causes a high-arched foot?

Causes include neurological conditions such as Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease, post-traumatic changes, congenital deformities, and cases with no identified cause. Identifying the cause is important for treatment planning.

Does every case of pes cavus involve a neurological condition?

No. However, neurological assessment is recommended if the deformity is progressive, one-sided, or associated with weakness. Examination to identify the cause is important.

Can insoles correct a high-arched foot?

Insoles do not correct the deformity, but may help reduce pain and calluses by distributing pressure over a wider area. Custom-made insoles are often more effective.

When is surgery needed for a high-arched foot?

Surgery may be considered for persistent pain, frequent sprains, or progressive deformity despite insoles, orthoses, and physical therapy. The plan depends on the deformity and underlying cause.

Can a high-arched foot cause frequent ankle sprains?

Yes. The heel may turn inward with a high-arched foot, which can reduce ankle stability and increase the risk of sprains. Foot structure should also be assessed in people with recurrent sprains.

Can pes cavus occur in children?

Yes. In children, pes cavus can sometimes be an early sign of a neurological condition. Paediatric orthopaedic and, if needed, neurological assessment is recommended for progressive or one-sided deformity.

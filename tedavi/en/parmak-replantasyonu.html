Finger Replantation: Kopan Parmak Nasıl Yeniden Dikilir?

Written and medically reviewed by: Assoc. Prof. Dr. Mesut Karıksız, Orthopaedics and Traumatology Specialist · Last updated: November 15, 2026

Finger replantation nedir? Finger replantation, tamamen kopmuş bir parmağın mikroskop altında kemik, damar, sinir ve tendonlarının onarılarak yeniden yerine dikilmesidir. Zaman çok önemlidir: kopma olduğunda kanamayı baskıyla durdurup parçayı doğru saklayarak en kısa sürede replantasyon yapabilen bir merkeze ulaşmak gerekir. Her kopan parmak dikilemeyebilir; uygunluk yaralanmanın şekline ve genel duruma göre değerlendirilir.

First aid when a finger is amputated

  1. Control bleeding: Apply direct pressure to the wound with a clean cloth and keep the arm elevated. Do not use a tourniquet.
  2. Protect the amputated part: Gently rinse the part with clean saline or water and wrap it in clean, damp gauze.
  3. Keep it cool: Place the wrapped part in a sealed bag and put it in a container with an ice-water mixture. Do not place it directly on ice or submerge it in water.
  4. Do not delay: Take the person and amputated part to a centre that can perform replantation as soon as possible. Calling ahead may help. Avoid eating or drinking in case anaesthesia is needed.

Do not place the amputated part directly on ice, immerse it in liquid, or apply heat. Acil durumda 112’yi arayın.

Can every amputated finger be replanted?

No. The decision depends on the type of injury, condition of the amputated part, and overall health.

Factors that may favour replantationFactors that may make replantation difficult or unsuitable
Clean, sharp cutsSevere crush or avulsion injuries
Amputation of the thumb, multiple fingers, or at palm levelInjury at multiple levels of the same finger
ChildrenOther life-threatening injuries
Proper cool storage of the amputated part and a short time since injurySevere contamination, prolonged time, or improper storage of the amputated part

How is the surgery performed?

  1. Cleaning the amputated part and wound, shortening the bone if needed, and fixation with a wire or plate
  2. Repair of the tendons
  3. Mikroskop altında suture of the arteries, veins, and nerves
  4. Closing the skin; a graft or flap may be used if needed

Surgery may take a long time; duration depends on the number of amputated fingers and injury complexity.

Recovery

  • After surgery, the patient is closely monitored for circulation (finger colour, temperature, and capillary refill)
  • If circulation problems occur, medication, repeat surgery, or specific medical interventions may be needed
  • Sigara ve nikotin damarlarda daralma yaparak başarıyı azaltır; kesinlikle bırakılmalıdır
  • Keeping the environment warm, elevating the arm, and protecting the wound are important
  • Splinting, hand therapy, and movement exercises may continue for months; nerve healing and return of sensation take a long time

Possible risks and expectations

  • Blood vessel blockage and failure of the replanted part to survive, which may result in loss of the finger
  • Stiffness, sensitivity to cold, or loss or change of sensation
  • Finger shortening or a change in appearance
  • Infection, bleeding, and the need for further surgery
  • Functional recovery varies with the type and level of amputation and rehabilitation; a full return to the previous condition cannot be guaranteed

When should you seek medical care?

  • Parmak veya elin herhangi bir parçası koptuğunda acilen sağlık kuruluşuna
  • Seek care without delay for partial amputation or deep cuts with darkening, coldness, or loss of sensation

Frequently asked questions

How soon should an amputated finger be replanted?

Time is critical, and surgery should be reached as soon as possible. Keeping the amputated part cool may extend the time window, but the exact time depends on the injury. Go to a hospital without delay.

Should I put an amputated finger on ice?

Do not put the part directly on ice. Wrap it in clean, damp gauze, place it in a bag, and put the bag in a container with an ice-water mixture. Do not submerge the part.

How long does replantation surgery take?

The duration depends on the number of amputated fingers and the complexity of the injury, and surgery may take hours. Details vary by patient and injury.

What is the chance of success with replantation?

Success depends on the type and level of amputation, time without blood supply, storage of the amputated part, and overall health. Success cannot be guaranteed for every patient.

Will the finger return to normal after replantation?

Even if the finger survives, movement, sensation, and appearance may not return to their previous state. Nerve healing and rehabilitation take months. Functional outcomes vary by person.

Can I smoke after replantation?

No. Nicotine constricts blood vessels, impairs circulation, and increases the risk that the replanted part will not survive. Avoid smoking throughout recovery.

Transforaminal Injection: An Interventional Treatment Around the Nerve Root

Written and medically reviewed by: Assoc. Prof. Mesut Kariksiz, Orthopaedic Surgeon · Last updated: November 15, 2026

Transforaminal (targeted) injection nedir? A transforaminal injection delivers medication through a fine needle to the opening (foramen) where a nerve root exits the spine, usually under fluoroscopic guidance. It may be used for diagnosis or treatment of leg pain (radiculopathy) related to a herniated disc or spinal stenosis. The medication is usually a corticosteroid and local anesthetic. Its effect is often temporary and does not remove the underlying cause; the aim is to reduce pain enough to support physical therapy.

When is it performed?

  • Nerve root pain (radiculopathy) radiating into one leg or arm due to a lumbar or cervical disc herniation
  • Pain caused by nerve root compression from spinal stenosis
  • For diagnosis: identifying which nerve root is causing the pain (selective nerve root block)
  • As a step before considering surgery when pain persists despite medication and physical therapy

An injection does not eliminate a disc herniation. Its purpose is to reduce inflammation and pain to support recovery and exercise. The duration of effect varies from person to person.

Who may not be a candidate?

  • People with an active infection at the injection site or elsewhere in the body
  • People taking blood thinners (medication adjustments must be directed by a physician) or with a bleeding disorder
  • People allergic to the medication or contrast agent
  • Pregnant people (because of radiation exposure)
  • Caution is needed for people with diabetes or high blood pressure because of corticosteroid effects
  • People with cauda equina symptoms or progressive weakness need urgent assessment rather than an injection

How is it performed?

  1. Preparation: The patient lies face down; the skin is cleaned and numbed.
  2. Imaging guidance: Fluoroscopy (live X-ray) or CT is used to guide the needle to the correct nerve root.
  3. Confirmation: A small amount of contrast confirms needle position and that it is not inside a blood vessel.
  4. Medication: A local anesthetic is given, usually together with a corticosteroid.
  5. Aftercare: Brief observation; the patient goes home the same day.

What should you expect afterward?

  • Temporary numbness or heaviness in the leg may occur due to local anesthesia; ask your physician before driving
  • The corticosteroid effect usually begins within days; mild soreness at the injection site may occur in the meantime
  • Physical therapy and exercise are started as pain improves; this opportunity is important for recovery
  • If relief is insufficient or short-lived, other options (repeat injection or endoscopic surgery) may be discussed
  • The number of injections is limited; follow your physician’s plan because of corticosteroid side effects

Possible risks

  • Pain or bruising at the injection site, or a temporary increase in pain
  • Temporary facial flushing, increased blood glucose or blood pressure, and insomnia related to corticosteroids
  • Enfeksiyon (nadir), kanama ve hematom (nadir)
  • Nerve injury (rare)
  • Very rare but serious complications include medication entering a blood vessel or blockage of spinal or brain vessels, potentially causing paralysis.   • Allergic reaction to medication or contrast agent

These risks are managed through appropriate patient selection, careful technique, and regular follow-up. Your individual risks will be discussed during your assessment.

When should you see an orthopaedic specialist?

  • Pain radiating into the leg or arm and lasting for weeks
  • Numbness, tingling, or weakness
  • Ağrı nedeniyle uyku ve günlük yaşamın bozulması
  • Any of the emergency symptoms listed above

Emergency: Seek emergency care immediately for weakness or numbness in both legs, loss of sensation around the groin or anus, or inability to control or pass urine or stool (cauda equina syndrome).

Frequently asked questions

What is a transforaminal (targeted) injection?

Halk arasında nokta atışı olarak bilinen yöntem, sinir kökünün çıktığı aralığa görüntüleme eşliğinde iğne ile ilaç verilmesidir. Doğru sinir kökünü hedeflediği için bu adı alır; tıbbi adı transforaminal epidural enjeksiyondur.

Is a transforaminal injection painful?

Cilt ve doku lokal anestezi ile uyuşturulur. İğne sinir köküne yaklaşırken bir miktar sızı veya bacağa vuran his olabilir. İşlem genellikle kısa sürer ve çoğu hasta iyi tolere eder.

How long do the effects last?

The duration varies from person to person; relief may last weeks or months for some, while others may not respond adequately. Relief does not mean the underlying cause has been eliminated; exercise and physical therapy are important during this period.

Kaç kez yapılabilir?

Because of corticosteroid side effects, the number of repeat injections is limited. The physician decides whether to repeat it based on the response to the first injection.

Can an injection prevent surgery?

Bazı hastalarda ağrıyı kontrol altına alarak ameliyat ihtiyacını azaltabilir veya geciktirebilir. Her hastada etkili olmaz; yanıt vermeyen veya ilerleyen şikâyeti olanlarda ameliyat düşünülür.

Diyabet hastası enjeksiyon yaptırabilir mi?

Corticosteroids may temporarily raise blood glucose. The injection may be an option for people with diabetes, but glucose monitoring and informing the physician are important.

Shoulder Muscle and Tendon (Rotator Cuff) Tears: Belirtiler, Tanı ve Tedavi

Written and medically reviewed by: Assoc. Prof. Dr. Mesut Karıksız, Orthopaedics and Traumatology Specialist · Last updated: November 15, 2026

Rotator cuff tear nedir? The rotator cuff is a group of muscles and tendons surrounding the shoulder joint that help lift and rotate the arm. A tear can cause shoulder pain, especially at night, and weakness when raising the arm. Causes may include age-related wear or trauma such as a fall. Treatment may be non-surgical or arthroscopic, depending on tear size, age, and individual needs.

What causes it?

  • Age-related tendon wear
  • Trauma such as a fall or force applied to the arm
  • Repeated overhead activity and heavy lifting

Symptoms

  • Shoulder pain, including night pain and difficulty lying on the affected side
  • Pain when raising the arm to the side or overhead
  • Weakness when raising the arm
  • Restricted movement
  • Grinding sensation or sound with arm movement

How is it diagnosed?

Muayenede omuz hareketleri ve kas gücü değerlendirilir. Ultrason ve MR yırtığın varlığını, boyutunu ve tendon kalitesini gösterir. Röntgen kemik yapıyı ve omuz çevresindeki kemik değişikliklerini değerlendirmeye yardımcı olur.

Treatment options

OptionWhen is it considered?How is it performed?
Non-surgical treatmentPartial tears, mild symptoms, or lower activity levelsRest, medication, physical therapy, and strengthening; injections for some patients at the doctor’s discretion
Arthroscopic rotator cuff repairFull-thickness tear, significant weakness, active patients, or lack of response to non-surgical treatmentThe tendon is reattached to the bone using sutures and anchors through a minimally invasive approach
Large or irreparable tearsWhen the tendon cannot be adequately repairedAlternative procedures, such as debridement or tendon transfer, as determined by the doctor

Not every tear requires surgery. Decisions consider tear size, age, activity level, severity of symptoms, and expectations.

After surgery

After repair, the shoulder is protected in a sling for a time, and movement is gradually introduced. Because tendon healing takes time, regaining strength and returning fully to daily activities usually take months.

Possible risks

  • Re-tear of the repaired tendon; the risk is higher with large tears
  • Shoulder stiffness
  • General surgical risks such as infection and bleeding
  • Temporary altered sensation around the surgical site

When should you see an orthopaedic specialist?

  • Shoulder pain lasting for weeks or disrupting sleep at night
  • Inability to raise the arm after a fall
  • Increasing weakness
  • Shoulder pain that limits daily activities

Frequently asked questions

Does a rotator cuff tear heal on its own?

Full-thickness tears usually do not heal on their own. However, pain and function may improve with physical therapy in some patients. Treatment decisions therefore depend on tear size and the person’s symptoms.

Does every rotator cuff tear require surgery?

No. Partial tears, mild symptoms, and lower activity levels may be managed without surgery. Surgery may be considered when weakness is significant, the patient is active, or non-surgical treatment has not helped.

Can an untreated rotator cuff tear get larger?

Some tears may enlarge over time, and tendon quality may deteriorate. Regular follow-up and assessment are important if symptoms persist.

Is shoulder pain always caused by a rotator cuff tear?

No. Other causes include impingement syndrome, calcific tendinitis, frozen shoulder, and pain originating in the neck. Diagnosis is based on examination and imaging.

When can I use my arm after surgery?

After repair, the shoulder is protected in a sling for a time, and movement is gradually introduced. The timing of return to daily activities and sports depends on tear size and healing and usually takes months.

Non-surgical treatment ne kadar sürede sonuç verir?

The effects of physical therapy and strengthening are generally assessed over weeks to months. Response varies from person to person and depends on the tear.
